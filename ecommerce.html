01

The Business Question

Where is sales value concentrated, and which customer or product groups should we investigate next? I approached the dataset as a commercial reporting problem: build one usable view, then break the total into understandable parts.

02

What I Built

I joined order items, orders, users, and products into a sales table in BigQuery. The saved source counts include 180,812 order items and 124,649 orders. These are different grains: an order can contain multiple items.

  • Aggregations for monthly sales, categories, countries, and customer spending.
  • Spend-based segments using CASE, plus product rankings within each country using a window function.
  • A Looker Studio dashboard and separate SQL queries for row counts, nulls, duplicates, and the date range.
03

A Finding, with Context

Outerwear & Coats and Jeans together account for 23.8% of the summed sales value in the saved category export. That calculation uses the category totals divided by the saved overall total of 10,778,116.37.

Outerwear & Coats12.4%

I would use that concentration to prioritize a margin and repeat-purchase analysis before recommending more budget or inventory. Sales value alone does not establish profitability or incremental demand.

04

How the SQL Works

The country-level ranking uses RANK() with a partition for each country. Equal sales values share a rank, so the result can include more than three products when there are ties.

RANK() OVER (
  PARTITION BY country
  ORDER BY SUM(sale_price) DESC
) AS rank_position
05

What I Would Check Before a Business Rollout

This is a public-data project, not an employer revenue result. The current totals sum sale_price across the joined data without filtering order status. I would agree the treatment of returns and cancellations before presenting a net-revenue metric.

  • Confirm the order-item key and test join cardinality to prevent duplicated totals.
  • Normalize country labels before comparing markets. The export includes both Spain and España.
  • Compare equal periods. The saved data ends in March 2026, so 2026 is a partial year.
